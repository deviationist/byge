In context
The first fifteen seconds of owning byge, in a real frame: the icon among other icons, the splash, then the answer. Every screen here is the shipping component at true phone size — nothing redrawn for the mockup — so the colours, the mark and the type sizes are the ones that ship.
1 · THE ICON, AMONG OTHER ICONS — WHERE IT HAS TO HOLD ITS OWN AT 60 PX
{{ i.label }}
A band crossing a point, at the only size that matters.
On a home screen the mark competes with everything else, at 60 px, usually against a dark wallpaper. Two solid shapes and a dot survive that; a cloud with a droplet would not. The deep blue ground reads as weather without being another blue-gradient weather icon — and it is the only place byge uses a saturated field, because inside the app that colour belongs to the rain.
Neighbours are blank placeholders on purpose: the question is whether ours holds up, not what it sits next to.
2 · TAP · THE SPLASH — A DOORWAY, NOT A WAIT
On screen for a few hundred milliseconds, and never longer.
This exists only to stop iOS flashing white before the cached verdict paints, so it holds nothing that needs finishing: no wordmark, no animation, no progress bar. The background is the app's own background — which is why the next frame does not feel like a page load. The field stays, the mark leaves, the answer arrives.
Same values as manifest background_color, per scheme: #F6F4F0 and #0E1113.

4 · THE LIST · ONE GLANCE, WHICH OF MY PLACES IS WET
Colour only where it carries meaning.
In a frame you can see how little colour there is: warm off-white or near-black, ink, and then the precipitation blues doing all of the signalling. The swatch column is the only saturated thing on screen, which is what makes it readable at arm's length — filled means raining now, outlined means on the way, hatched means we cannot see.
Dark uses the second ramp: luminance rises with intensity, so heavy rain is bright rather than dim.
